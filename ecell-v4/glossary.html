Guide Startup glossary

Speak startup.

Plain-English meanings of the words founders, mentors and investors use. The examples follow Campus Cabs, an imaginary student startup.

ABCDEFGIKLMNPRSTUV

A

A/B testingSplit testingProduct
Comparing two versions of something, such as a sign-up page, by showing each to a separate, randomly chosen group of users and measuring which works better. Change one thing at a time so the result is clear.
Campus Cabs randomly shows half its new users a 'Share a ride' button and half 'Split the fare', then compares bookings.
AcceleratorFunding & equity
A fixed-length programme that takes in a group of startups and helps them grow quickly through mentoring, workshops and investor introductions. Many accelerators invest a small amount for equity, and the programme usually ends with a demo day.
Campus Cabs joins an accelerator to sharpen its business model and meet investors at the closing demo day.
Angel investorAngelFunding & equity
An individual who invests their own money in an early-stage startup, usually in exchange for equity. Many angels also offer advice and useful contacts.
A retired transport business owner invests in Campus Cabs and helps the founders negotiate with local drivers.

B

Beta testingBetaProduct
Releasing an almost-finished product to a limited group of real users so you can find bugs and collect feedback before launching it to everyone.
Campus Cabs opens its app to one hostel first and fixes booking problems before inviting the whole campus.
BootstrappingFunding & equity
Building a startup with the founders' own savings and the money it earns from customers, without outside investment. The founders keep full ownership and control, but growth is limited by the cash they have.
Campus Cabs pays for its first app version from the founders' savings and early ride fares.
Break-evenBreak-even pointBusiness model & metrics
The point where total revenue equals total costs, so the business makes neither a profit nor a loss. Above this point it makes a profit; below it, a loss.
Campus Cabs breaks even in any month where fare income exactly matches its total costs: driver pay, app costs, salaries and marketing.
Burn rateBurnBusiness model & metrics
How fast a startup spends its cash, usually measured per month. Gross burn is total monthly spending. Net burn is spending minus the cash coming in from customers: the money actually lost each month.
Campus Cabs' total monthly spending is its gross burn; subtract its fare income to get its net burn.
Business-to-businessB2BMarket & customers
A business model in which a company sells its product or service to other businesses rather than to individual people. Sales often take longer and involve several decision-makers, but each customer is often worth more.
If Campus Cabs sold staff transport contracts to colleges, that part of its business would be B2B.
Business-to-consumerB2CMarket & customers
A business model in which a company sells directly to individual people for their own use. It usually needs to reach many customers, so marketing and ease of use matter a great deal.
Campus Cabs is B2C when students book and pay for their own seats in the app.

C

Cap tableCapitalisation tableFunding & equity
A table showing who owns what in a company: founders, investors and the employee option pool, with each one's shares and percentage. It is updated whenever ownership changes.
After its first angel round, the Campus Cabs cap table lists both founders, the angel and an employee option pool.
ChurnChurn rateBusiness model & metrics
The share of customers who stop using or paying for your product during a given period. High churn means you must keep winning new customers just to stay the same size.
If many students who booked in August stop booking by October, Campus Cabs has a churn problem.
Co-founderCompany & legal basics
One of the people who start a company together, sharing ownership, responsibility and risk from the early days. Strong founding teams tend to combine different skills with shared values.
One Campus Cabs co-founder builds the app; the other signs up drivers and runs daily operations.
Convertible noteFunding & equity
A short-term loan from an investor that converts into shares later, usually at the next priced round, often with a discount or valuation cap. Until it converts, it is debt, usually with interest and a repayment date.
An angel lends Campus Cabs money now; the loan becomes shares when the startup raises its seed round.
Customer acquisition costCACBusiness model & metrics
The average cost of winning one new customer. Add up what you spent on sales and marketing in a period and divide it by the number of new customers you gained.
Campus Cabs adds up a month's posters, referral credits and ambassador fees, then divides by that month's new riders.
Customer discoveryIdeas & validation
Talking to potential customers to understand their problems, habits and needs before building much. Ask about what they actually did in the past, not whether they like your idea.
The Campus Cabs founders ask students how they last travelled to the city, what it cost and what went wrong.

D

Demo dayPitching & growth
An event, usually at the end of an accelerator or incubator programme, where startups present their progress and pitch to investors, mentors and guests.
Campus Cabs uses its demo day slot to show ride growth and invite investors to follow up.
DilutionFunding & equity
The drop in an existing shareholder's ownership percentage when the company issues new shares. You keep the same number of shares, but they form a smaller slice of a company that is ideally worth more.
When an angel buys new shares in Campus Cabs, each founder's percentage falls even though their share count stays the same.
Direct-to-consumerD2C, DTCMarket & customers
A brand that sells its own products straight to customers, usually online, instead of through shops, distributors or other middlemen. It controls pricing and the customer relationship but must find customers itself.
Due diligenceFunding & equity
The careful checking an investor does before investing. They review your finances, legal documents, ownership, intellectual property and the claims you made in your pitch.
Before investing, an angel asks Campus Cabs for its company documents, driver agreements, bank statements and ride records.

E

Early adoptersIdeas & validation
The first customers who feel the problem strongly enough to try a new, imperfect product. Their feedback shapes the product, and their use is early evidence that people want it.
Students who often travel to the city late in the evening are Campus Cabs' likely early adopters.
Elevator pitchPitching & growth
A short, clear spoken summary of what your startup does, who it is for and why it matters. It should be brief enough to finish during a lift ride.
Campus Cabs' elevator pitch: safe, affordable shared rides for students between campus and the city, booked in a few taps.
Employee stock option planESOPCompany & legal basics
A plan that gives employees options: the right to buy company shares later at a fixed price, once the options have vested. Startups use it to attract and keep people when they cannot pay high salaries.
Campus Cabs sets aside a pool of shares to offer options to its first engineers.
EquityFunding & equity
Ownership in a company, usually held as shares. The founders typically start with all of it, then share some with employees and investors in exchange for work or money.
The Campus Cabs founders split the equity between them, then offer some to an angel in exchange for funding.

F

Feature creepProduct
The steady addition of extra features that delays launch and makes a product harder to use, without solving the core problem any better.
Adding in-app games and chat to Campus Cabs before booking works reliably is feature creep.
Founders' agreementCo-founder agreementCompany & legal basics
A written agreement between co-founders covering roles, equity split, vesting, decision-making, ownership of intellectual property and what happens if someone leaves. Write it early, while everyone gets along.
The Campus Cabs founders agree in writing what happens to unvested shares if one of them leaves.
FreemiumBusiness model & metrics
A pricing model where the basic product is free and customers pay for extra features or higher limits. The free version attracts users, and a smaller group pays for more.

G

Go-to-market strategyGTMPitching & growth
Your plan for reaching customers and selling to them when you launch a product or enter a new market: who you target, which channels you use, how you price and what you say.
Campus Cabs launches in one hostel through student ambassadors and referral codes before expanding across campus.
Gross marginBusiness model & metrics
Revenue minus the direct costs of delivering your product, divided by revenue and shown as a percentage. It shows how much of each sale is left to cover other costs, such as salaries and marketing.
For Campus Cabs, gross margin is the share of each fare left after paying the driver and payment fees.
Growth hackingPitching & growth
Using low-cost, creative experiments to grow users quickly, often built into the product itself, such as referral rewards. Each idea is tested fast, measured and kept only if it works.
Campus Cabs gives both the rider and their friend a discount when a referral code is used.

I

Ideal customer profileICPMarket & customers
A clear description of the kind of customer who gets the most value from your product and is most likely to pay. It helps you focus effort instead of trying to sell to everyone.
For Campus Cabs: hostel students without their own vehicle who travel to the city every week.
IncubatorFunding & equity
An organisation, often linked to a university, that supports very early startups with workspace, mentoring, networks and sometimes funding. Support usually lasts longer and is less intensive than an accelerator.
Campus Cabs uses a university incubator's workspace and mentors while building its first app.
Intellectual propertyIPCompany & legal basics
Creations of the mind that a business can own, such as inventions, brand names, logos, designs, software code and written content. Patents, trademarks, copyright and trade secrets protect different kinds.
Campus Cabs' intellectual property includes its name, logo and ride-matching software.
IterationProduct
Improving a product in repeated short cycles: build something, put it in front of users, learn from their response and change it. Each cycle is one iteration.
After riders complain about waiting, Campus Cabs adds live driver tracking, then checks whether complaints fall.

K

Key performance indicatorKPIBusiness model & metrics
A measurable number that shows progress towards an important goal. Pick a few that truly matter and track them regularly, rather than measuring everything.
Campus Cabs tracks weekly rides, repeat riders and average waiting time as its KPIs.

L

Lifetime valueLTV, CLVBusiness model & metrics
What one customer is expected to earn you over the whole time they stay, ideally counted as gross profit, not revenue. If it is below customer acquisition cost, each new customer loses you money.
Campus Cabs estimates the gross profit from a typical student's rides across their whole degree, not just one trip.
Limited liability partnershipLLPCompany & legal basics
A business structure that combines a partnership's flexibility with limited liability, so partners' personal assets are generally protected from the business's debts. It has no shares, so equity investors usually prefer a private limited company.

M

MarketplaceBusiness model & metrics
A platform that connects two groups, usually buyers and sellers, and typically earns a fee or commission on each transaction. It must attract both sides at once, which is hard at the start.
Campus Cabs is a marketplace if it connects students with independent local drivers and keeps a fee per ride.
MentorPitching & growth
An experienced person who guides you with advice, feedback and introductions, often without being paid. Listen carefully, but the decisions remain yours as the founder.
A mentor who once ran a logistics business helps Campus Cabs plan driver schedules.
Minimum viable productMVPProduct
The simplest version of your product that real users can use, built to test your main idea and learn as quickly and cheaply as possible. It is a learning tool, not a smaller final product.
Before building an app, Campus Cabs matches riders through a messaging group and a shared spreadsheet.

N

Non-disclosure agreementNDACompany & legal basics
A contract in which people agree to keep shared information confidential. Many investors decline to sign one before a pitch, so share only what you are comfortable sharing.
Campus Cabs asks a freelance developer to sign an NDA before seeing its ride-matching logic.
North star metricPitching & growth
The single number that best captures the value your product delivers to customers. The team focuses on growing it because, if it is chosen well, lasting revenue growth should follow.
Campus Cabs might choose completed shared rides per week as its north star metric.

P

Pain pointIdeas & validation
A specific problem or frustration that customers experience and would like solved. The stronger and more frequent the pain, the more likely people are to pay for a solution.
The pain point Campus Cabs targets: finding a safe, affordable ride from the city back to campus late in the evening.
PatentCompany & legal basics
A legal right to stop others making, using or selling your invention, granted separately in each country or region for a limited period. The invention must be new, non-obvious and useful, and its details are made public in return.
A patent could protect a genuinely new invention Campus Cabs creates, but not its name or logo, which trademarks cover.
PersonaUser personaMarket & customers
A short, realistic sketch of a typical user, with a name, background, goals and frustrations, based on real research. It helps the team remember who they are building for.
Campus Cabs' main persona is Priya, a hostel student who travels to the city every weekend and dislikes bargaining over late-night fares.
Pitch deckPitching & growth
A short set of slides that explains your startup to investors, usually covering the problem, solution, market, competition, business model, traction, team and what you are asking for.
The Campus Cabs deck ends by stating how much the founders want to raise and what they will spend it on.
PivotIdeas & validation
A deliberate change in a key part of your strategy, such as the customer, problem, product or business model, based on what you have learnt. You keep what works and change what does not.
If staff ride daily but students rarely do, Campus Cabs might pivot to serving staff commutes.
Pre-money and post-money valuationFunding & equity
Pre-money valuation is the agreed value of a company just before new investment. Post-money valuation is the pre-money value plus the new money invested. An investor's ownership is their investment divided by the post-money valuation.
Campus Cabs and an angel agree a pre-money valuation; adding the angel's investment gives the post-money valuation, which sets the angel's percentage.
Pre-seedFunding & equity
The earliest stage of funding, often from the founders, family, friends or angel investors, used to build a first version and test the idea. Stage names are loose and mean different things to different investors.
Private limited companyPvt LtdCompany & legal basics
A company with its own legal identity, separate from its shareholders, whose liability is limited to what they agreed to pay for their shares. It cannot offer shares to the public and is the usual choice for startups raising investment.
The founders register Campus Cabs as a private limited company so it can issue shares to an angel and offer employee options.
Problem-solution fitIdeas & validation
The stage where you have evidence that a real problem exists for a specific group of people and that your solution addresses it. It comes before product-market fit.
In interviews, students confirm late-night travel is a real problem, and many sign up to try Campus Cabs' shared rides.
Product-market fitPMFIdeas & validation
When your product satisfies strong demand in a real market: customers keep using it, recommend it to others and would be genuinely disappointed if it disappeared.
Students book Campus Cabs week after week and bring friends without being asked.
PrototypeProduct
An early, often rough model of a product, built to test how an idea looks, feels or works. Unlike an MVP, it is usually not used by real customers in everyday conditions.
The Campus Cabs founders show classmates clickable screens of the booking app before writing any real code.

R

RetentionBusiness model & metrics
How well you keep customers using your product over time. Strong retention is one of the clearest signs that people value what you have built.
Campus Cabs checks how many students who rode in their first week are still riding a month later.
Revenue vs profitBusiness model & metrics
Revenue is money earned from sales, before any costs are taken off; money from investors is not revenue. Profit is what remains after all costs are subtracted, so a startup can grow revenue quickly and still make a loss.
Campus Cabs can collect plenty of fares yet lose money after paying drivers, salaries and marketing.
RoadmapProduct roadmapProduct
A plan showing what the team intends to build and in roughly what order, linked to the product's goals. It should change as you learn from users.
Campus Cabs plans booking first, then payments, then scheduled rides, and revisits the order as it learns from riders.
RunwayBusiness model & metrics
How long a startup can keep operating before its cash runs out at the current burn rate. Divide the cash in the bank by the monthly net burn to estimate it in months.
Campus Cabs extends its runway by cutting marketing spend, which lowers its monthly net burn.

S

ScalabilityProduct
The ability to serve many more customers without costs and effort growing at the same pace. A scalable business can become more efficient as it grows.
Campus Cabs' booking software can serve another campus cheaply; signing up drivers in a new city takes far more work.
Seed roundSeedFunding & equity
Usually the first sizeable round of outside investment, used to find product-market fit, build the team and start growing. It often comes from angels and early-stage venture funds.
Series AFunding & equity
The first large funding round after seed, usually led by a venture capital firm. Startups raise it once they have strong traction, signs of product-market fit and a credible plan to grow.
Simple agreement for future equitySAFEFunding & equity
An agreement where an investor pays now for shares later, usually at the next priced round, often with a discount or valuation cap. Unlike a convertible note, it is not a loan and has no interest or repayment date.
Before offering an angel a SAFE, the Campus Cabs founders check with a lawyer that local law allows it.
Software as a serviceSaaSBusiness model & metrics
Software that customers use over the internet and pay for regularly, usually through a subscription, instead of buying and installing it themselves.
If Campus Cabs sold its ride-scheduling software to other colleges for a monthly fee, that would be SaaS.
Sole proprietorshipCompany & legal basics
A business owned and run by one person, with no legal separation between the owner and the business. It is simple to start, but the owner is personally responsible for all its debts.
SubscriptionBusiness model & metrics
A model where customers pay a regular fee, such as monthly or yearly, for continued access to a product or service. It gives the business more predictable income.
Campus Cabs offers a monthly pass covering weekday rides between campus and the city.

T

TAM, SAM and SOMTotal addressable, serviceable available and serviceable obtainable marketMarket & customers
Three nested estimates of market size. TAM is the total demand for your kind of product. SAM is the part your business model and reach can serve. SOM is the part of SAM you can realistically win soon.
TAM: student travel between campuses and cities nationwide. SAM: that travel at campuses Campus Cabs can reach. SOM: the share it can realistically win soon.
Term sheetFunding & equity
A short document setting out the main terms of an investment, such as the amount, valuation and investor rights. Most of it is not legally binding, though clauses such as confidentiality and exclusivity often are. Detailed legal agreements follow.
TractionPitching & growth
Evidence that people want your product, such as growing users, revenue, repeat use, pre-orders or signed partnerships. Investors look for it because it shows progress beyond the idea.
Campus Cabs shows steady weekly growth in rides and many repeat riders from its first hostel.
TrademarkCompany & legal basics
A name, logo, slogan or other sign that identifies your brand's goods or services. Registering it helps you stop others using something confusingly similar, and unlike a patent, the registration can usually be renewed indefinitely.
Before applying to register the name Campus Cabs, the founders check that no similar mark is already registered or used for transport services.

U

Unique selling propositionUSP, unique selling pointMarket & customers
The one clear thing that makes your product different from, and better than, the alternatives for your customers. It should be specific, true and easy to remember.
Campus Cabs: shared rides timed to college schedules, with verified drivers, costing less than a solo taxi.
Unit economicsBusiness model & metrics
The revenue and costs linked to a single unit of your business, such as one customer, order or ride. It tells you whether each sale makes or loses money before you try to scale.
Campus Cabs compares what one shared ride earns with what it costs in driver pay, payment fees and support.

V

ValidationIdeas & validation
Testing whether your key assumptions about the problem, customers and willingness to pay are true. Use evidence from real behaviour, such as sign-ups, pre-orders or payments, rather than opinions.
Campus Cabs takes paid advance bookings for one weekend ride before building the app, to see if students will actually pay.
ValuationFunding & equity
What a company is considered to be worth, usually agreed between founders and investors during a funding round. For early startups it is negotiated, not calculated precisely.
Value propositionMarket & customers
A clear statement of the benefit customers get from your product and why it serves them better than their current options.
Campus Cabs: reach the city safely and cheaply, without waiting or bargaining, by sharing a ride with fellow students.
Venture capitalVCFunding & equity
Investment from firms that pool money from others and put it into high-growth startups in exchange for equity. They expect many investments to fail and a few large successes to make up for the losses.
Campus Cabs might approach venture capital firms once it has strong traction across several campuses.
VestingCompany & legal basics
Earning shares or options gradually instead of all at once. A cliff is an initial period in which nothing vests; when it ends, a first portion vests together. Anyone who leaves early usually keeps only what has vested.
A Campus Cabs co-founder who leaves before the cliff ends keeps none of their shares, because none have vested yet.
